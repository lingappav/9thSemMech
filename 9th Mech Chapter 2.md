## 2. Historical & Legal Context: The End of Dynastic Marriage in India

### Strategic Alliances in Imperial History
Historically, *Vivaha Sambandha* (matrimonial alliance) was the primary non-violent tool for territorial consolidation in India:
* **Diplomatic Defense:** Securing borders and turning potential invaders into grandfathers of future heirs (e.g., Chandragupta Maurya & Seleucus Nicator's line; Akbar's Rajput alliances).
* **Feudal Integration:** Absorbing regional chieftains (*Samantas*) into the royal court to guarantee tax revenue and military loyalty.

### Why Polygamy Was Abolished in India
1. **Socio-Religious Reforms:** 19th-century movements led by Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Swami Dayananda Saraswati, and Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar combated practices like *Kulin* hypergamy to protect women's economic rights and human dignity.
2. **Dissolution of Princely States (1947–1948):** The integration of over 500 princely states eliminated the royal *zenana* system and its fiscal infrastructure.
3. **Statutory Monogamy Mandate (Hindu Marriage Act, 1955):** Section 5(i) enforced monogamy, while Sections 11 and 17 criminalized bigamy under Section 494 of the Indian Penal Code.
4. **Closing Judicial Loopholes:** Supreme Court rulings in *Sarla Mudgal (1995)* and *Lily Thomas (2000)* established that converting to another religion without dissolving a first marriage remains criminal bigamy.

* **Demographic Imbalance ("Bachelor Surplus"):** Because the human birth ratio is naturally 1:1, a small group of wealthy men hoarding female partners leaves a large pool of low-income men involuntarily single.
* **Elevated Crime & Instability:** Sociological and evolutionary research confirms that large pools of disenfranchised, unmarried men lead directly to higher rates of violent crime, risky behavior, and political radicalization.
* **Diluted Parental Attention:** Financial wealth cannot expand time; dividing a single father's hours across dozens or hundreds of children dilutes emotional investment and often triggers severe multi-generational inheritance disputes.
